Why is yoga so important as we age?

By Selin

There is a quiet assumption that getting older means slowly doing less: moving less, bending less, trying less. On the mat, I see something very different. Some of the most curious, committed students in my classes are in their fifties and sixties, and they keep surprising themselves.

Strength you can actually use

Yoga builds strength in a very practical way. Holding a warrior pose, lowering slowly from plank or rising from a lunge asks your muscles to support you through real-life movements. That is the same strength you use to carry shopping, climb stairs or get up off the floor after playing with the grandkids.

Moving through your full range

Day to day, most of us move in a small range: sitting, standing, walking, reaching for the same shelf. Over time the body adapts to what we ask of it. A yoga class gently invites the hips, spine and shoulders to move in every direction again, so movement keeps feeling available rather than effortful.

Steadiness and balance

Balance is a skill, and like any skill it improves with practice. Standing poses, slow transitions and simple balances on one leg all train the small stabilising muscles and the connection between your feet and your brain. Many students tell me they notice it first on uneven ground or when they turn quickly.

A calmer nervous system

Every class includes time to slow the breath and settle. That pause matters. It gives you a chance to notice how you feel, let go of the day and leave a little lighter than you arrived.

Connection

With only six people in each class at Bloom, nobody is a face in the crowd. Familiar faces turn into friends, and that sense of belonging is one of the best reasons to keep coming back.

You do not need to be flexible to start. You only need to begin where you are. If you have an injury or a health condition, check in with your doctor or physio first, and let me know before class so I can offer the right options for you.
