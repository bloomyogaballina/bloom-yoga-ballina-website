How mobility supports you

By Selin

Mobility is one of those words that gets used a lot. In simple terms, it is your ability to move a joint actively and comfortably through its full range, under your own control.

Mobility is not the same as flexibility

Flexibility is how far a muscle can be stretched. Mobility adds strength and control to that range. You might be able to pull your leg up high with your hands, but can you lift it there and hold it on its own? That second part is mobility, and it is what helps in daily life.

Why it matters every day

Good mobility makes ordinary things feel easy: turning to look over your shoulder while driving, reaching into a high cupboard, getting in and out of the car, crouching down in the garden. When a joint stops moving through its full range, other parts of the body often start working harder to make up for it.

How yoga builds mobility

In class we move the spine in every direction, open the hips and shoulders, and spend time in poses that ask you to be both soft and strong. Slow, controlled transitions are where a lot of the magic happens, so you will hear me cue them carefully.

A few simple ideas to try

  • Roll your shoulders slowly forwards and backwards a few times each morning.
  • Try a gentle cat-cow on hands and knees to wake up the spine.
  • Spend a minute or two in a supported squat, holding onto a bench or doorframe if you need to.
  • Take a short walk and let your arms swing freely.

Little and often beats long and rare. A few minutes of mindful movement each day can make a real difference to how your body feels. And if anything hurts, ease off and check in with a health professional.
